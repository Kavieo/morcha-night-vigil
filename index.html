MORCHA :: NIGHT VIGIL

BORDER OBSERVATION & INFILTRATION-DETECTION TRAINER — PUNJAB / JAMMU IB SECTOR

You are the sentry in an elevated morcha on the Anti-Infiltration Obstacle System. You hold this post's assigned arc — the embrasure gives you 58° either side and the flanking morchas hold the rest. Your shift is one compressed night — five minutes. Across the zero line there is scrub, elephant grass and a nallah, and behind that the Pakistan Rangers BOPs, lit, with their towers up, watching you back. Some of what moves out there is cattle. Some of it is a man on his belly.

You cannot watch the whole arc. That is the exercise. Everything below follows from it.

HOW YOU WORK THIS

You do not touch the machine. You watch the screen with your own eyes, exactly as you would watch your ground.

The moment you have something, press SPACE. Then point at it on the screen — hand or laser — and give the bearing out loud.

The instructor confirms whether you were right, and works the radio, the light and the QRT as you call for them. Ask for binoculars or thermal by name; he will put them up for you.

WHY IT IS DONE THIS WAY

A sentry does not aim a mouse. He looks, he decides, he reports and he points.

Pressing the button marks the exact second you saw it. That second is what you are judged on at the end — against the second the contact first became visible, and where your arc was pointing at the time.

HOW YOU ACTUALLY DETECT HIM

You will not see the man. At 250 m a prone crawler is a few pixels.
You will see the grass. Wind is steady and from one direction — watch the bearing tape. A patch of grass working across the prevailing wind, in short bursts, is a body moving through it.

He moves when your arc is elsewhere and freezes the moment you settle on him. Sweeping fast reveals nothing. Sweeping slowly leaves the rest of the arc blind.

THE GROUND WILL TELL YOU WHERE

Dead floodlights. Three lamps on the fence are u/s. Those dark gaps are marked U/S on your bearing tape. That is where a trained infiltrator goes.

The nallah. The culvert under the fence on the left flank is dead ground the whole way in.

Noise is a lie. If you hear movement on one flank, ask what it is pulling you off.

YOUR SIGHTS ALL COST YOU SOMETHING

You watch with your own eyes. Ask the instructor for anything else by name.

Naked eye — wide arc, blind past 150 m.
Binocular — detail and the only way to confirm a weapon, but a 7° field.
Thermal — sees heat through grass, and cannot tell a farmer from an infiltrator.
Image intensifier — detail at night, and blooms out for 4 seconds if your own flare catches it.

THE LADDER IS NOT OPTIONAL

Observe → report grid → challenge → illuminate → QRT → warning shot → aimed fire.

Call each one out loud. The instructor works the radio, the loudhailer, the light, the QRT and the rifle for you — in the order you call them.

The QRT takes 42 seconds to reach a grid. A crawler's final dash at the wire is quicker than that. You must commit your reserve on a suspicion, and it arrives where you said, not where he is.

WHAT IS ACROSS THE LINE

Three Rangers BOPs sit 200–300 m inside Pakistan, marked PAK on your bearing tape. Their lamps burn all night. They are a fixed reference, a permanent distraction, and the reason for the hardest rule you hold:

Rounds do not cross the International Boundary. Anything still beyond the zero line is on Pakistani soil, whatever it is doing. You may engage a man in no-man's land. You may not engage the same man thirty metres further back.

WHAT IS BEHIND YOU

The A Coy patrol walks the track on our own side of the wire, twice a shift. Armed, upright, moving with purpose. After ten minutes of staring into the far scrub you will want to designate them.

Challenge before you do anything else — they answer with the password. Aimed fire on them ends the shift and your career.
CONSEQUENCE. One infiltrator through the fence fails the shift. Aimed fire on an unarmed man — a cultivator near the zero line, a smuggler with his hands empty — is a Court of Inquiry, and it scores worse than a breach. Rounds across the IB is a cross-border firing incident. Rounds at our own patrol is fratricide. You will be shown, at the end, the exact minute the grass moved and where your arc was pointing at that moment.
